# State your level, then prove it

The best engineer portfolios of 2025-2026 are short, text-first indexes that say who the person is, at what level, and what proves it before the reader scrolls, and they spend one visual idea where it never blocks that reading. Tania Rascia's four-row timeline, Brittany Chiang's sticky identity column, Anthony Fu's role-labelled project chips, Mitchell Hashimoto's one-screen career and Delba de Oliveira's stated job search are the strongest models. Paco Coursey, Jhey Tompkins and Rauno Freiberg prove craft while still saying who they are, and Josh Comeau, Dan Abramov and Sam Rose show that writing can be the portfolio. Spectacle sites such as Bruno Simon's are real achievements and poor models for anyone who must be understood quickly. A portfolio matters less than the advice industry implies: no study of any size measures how reviewers read an engineer's site, and the available evidence casts it as late confirmation, not an entry ticket. It matters more for a frontend specialist, because the site is itself a work sample. The open ground is specificity: **no page reviewed gave a quantified business or product outcome for employer work**, and AI has made polish cheap, so dated, attributable proof now separates a site from its template. For nicholaspsmith.com the order follows from this: fix the broken basics first (direct links return 404, a 45 MB video loads on every visit), then turn the stock rain into a designed system.

## A portfolio confirms a decision more often than it starts one

The evidence base is thin, and any confident number about portfolio reading is suspect. **No eye-tracking, click-through or dwell-time data on software portfolio sites turned up**; all timing data concerns resumes, and all structured portfolio data concerns UX designers. Everything below on site behaviour is extrapolation or practitioner opinion.

The resume data is still instructive. In interviewing.io's 2024 study, 76 technical recruiters made nearly 2,200 paid evaluations of over 1,000 engineers' resumes against mock-interview ground truth. **Median time per resume was 31 seconds, recruiters were right 55% of the time, and FAANG or FAANG-adjacent experience made a candidate 35% more likely to be picked** ([interviewing.io](https://interviewing.io/blog/are-recruiters-better-than-a-coin-flip-at-judging-resumes)). Ladders' older vendor study reported a 7.4-second first screen spent on titles and subheadings, with clutter, multiple columns and missing headers performing poorly; only secondary coverage was reachable ([HR Dive](https://www.hrdive.com/news/recruiters-spend-74-seconds-looking-at-your-resume/541582/)). The two figures measure different things, a rapid sort and a paid careful judgement, and agree on the point that matters: the first reader decides in under a minute, using titles, employers and structure.

Whether that reader opens a portfolio at all is doubtful. Gergely Orosz's July 2026 reporting, drawn from more than 50 interviews, describes hiring that runs on referrals and direct outreach, with one director saying "we gave up on inbound hiring" and thin online presence making outreach harder ([Pragmatic Engineer](https://newsletter.pragmaticengineer.com/p/tech-jobs-market-in-2026-part-3-hiring)). Al Sweigart's 2026 poll of self-described hirers drew replies such as "They rarely land you the job or play a part in the screening", with one dissent, "If it's good, definitely" ([r/cscareerquestions snapshot](https://reddit.sentinel-team.org/posts/1t4gswl/snapshots/2026-05-05T19%3A04%3A38.345544Z)). The clearest split came from another thread: screeners are unlikely to check, but "likely at least one curious interviewer would have taken a quick look" ([r/cscareerquestionsCAD snapshot](https://reddit.sentinel-team.org/posts/1p8tj1u/snapshots/2025-12-06T02%3A11%3A22.504Z)). These threads skew junior and self-selected, so they establish a direction, not a rate.

The defensible conclusion is that a senior portfolio has four jobs: something a referrer can forward, something a searcher can find, material for an interview, and a late tiebreaker. None of these is "get past the recruiter". The risk also looks asymmetric, because reviewers arrive to confirm or eliminate, so a broken or stale site can cost more than a good one gains. Frontend is the main exception to the general scepticism. Holloway notes that personal sites can reveal frontend skill where relevant ([Holloway](https://holloway.com/g/technical-recruiting-hiring/sections/linkedin-github-and-personal-websites)), and one guide names frontend, design and product engineering as the roles that benefit most, without evidence ([techinterview.org](https://www.techinterview.org/post/3233474627/personal-website-portfolio-engineers/)).

Three readers use the same site at different depths, which is why one site can serve hiring managers and developers. This layering is reasoned, not tested.

| Reader | Mode | Wants | Convinced by |
|---|---|---|---|
| Recruiter or sourcer | Seconds, scanning | Relevance, seniority, location, a way to reach you | Titles, employers, dates, plain summary, resume link |
| Hiring manager | Minutes, late in the process | Scope, judgement, business results, risk | Two or three case studies with role, trade-offs, outcomes |
| Peer engineer | Reads deep if interested | Taste, honesty, technical substance | Code, write-ups, failure stories, a fast and well-made site |

The real conflict is between demonstration and access: peers may enjoy cleverness that delays a recruiter. Keeping any flourish off the path to the core facts resolves most of it. NN/g's January 2026 warning from design hiring applies directly: readers "are trying to solve their own problems", and writing to impress peers is a red flag ([NN/g](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/ux-hiring-insights/)).

## Three juries reward different sites, and none of them measures hiring

No single "best" list exists, because three separate audiences reward different things. Hacker News rewards writing: simonwillison.net drew 2,577 HN comment mentions, jvns.ca 460 and overreacted.io 226, against 1 for rauno.me and 0 for brittanychiang.com, counted through the [Algolia HN API](https://hn.algolia.com/api). That is crude string matching and it undercounts design engineers, whose audience sits on X. GitHub rewards reusable layout: Brittany Chiang's previous site repo has **8,292 stars and 4,195 forks** ([bchiang7/v4](https://github.com/bchiang7/v4)). Awwwards rewards spectacle by construction, weighting **Design 40%, Usability 30%, Creativity 20% and Content 10%** ([Awwwards](https://www.awwwards.com/about-evaluation/)), so a high score says little about whether a site explains a career. Its portfolio listing is dominated by individual creatives and small studios, not software engineers ([Awwwards listing](https://www.awwwards.com/websites/portfolio/)).

Each exemplar below was observed on 2026-11-05 through text extraction, raw source, headless capture at 1280×800 and computed-style measurement. The verdicts are judgement against those observations, since no source shows which of these sites produced a hire. Hover and keyboard behaviour were read from source, not exercised by hand.

The sites that fail as models fail for the same reason: they make the reader work before learning anything. **Bruno Simon's driving world won Awwwards Site of the Day on 21 January 2026 at 8.11, with accessibility the lowest of its Dev Award sub-scores at 6.60** ([Awwwards](https://www.awwwards.com/sites/brunos-portfolio)). A software-GL capture showed no name or text after 14 seconds, though real GPU behaviour was not tested, and the 777-point HN thread mixed acclaim with reports of frozen tabs and near-100% CPU on an M3 ([HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=46206531)). It suits creative-developer roles. Hamish Williams's site showed an error page, "Flatlined / Error: Error creating WebGL context", with the GPU disabled and no fallback content ([hamishw.com](https://hamishw.com)). OS-simulator sites draw praise, including 683 points for one macOS clone ([HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=27084995)), but the praise is for the build and nothing reports a hire.

Partial cases teach the conditions. Maxime Heckel's first viewport has no plain sentence about who he is and runs to 15,135px, redeemed because WebGL is his specialism ([maximeheckel.com](https://maximeheckel.com)). Addy Osmani's wall works for someone with about 19 books and would hurt anyone with less. The minimalism of Lee Robinson, Emil Kowalski and Shu Ding is elegant but borrows an existing reputation, so it is risky to copy without one. Upkeep is a real failure mode too: Cassie Evans has replaced her celebrated site with a farewell note ([cassie.codes](https://cassie.codes)), and Steve Ruiz's newest post dated from November 2023 when checked ([steveruiz.me](https://www.steveruiz.me)).

## Twenty sites converge on a short index, one accent and one idea

Across 20 hand-picked sites, the structure is more uniform than the visual variety suggests. These counts are one observer's classification, so they show tendencies, not rates. **Seven home pages run under 1,500px, eight between 1,500 and 6,000px, and only three beyond that**; the remaining two could not be measured. Nearly all are hybrids, a home index linking to work and writing pages, and 13 of 20 surface writing on the home page. Eight keep navigation persistent, and the left column recurs among the most communicative sites (Brittany, Tania, Mitchell). Lee, Paco and Emil have no nav at all because the page is short enough not to need one.

The type and colour consensus is narrow. Body text measured 587-656px wide on most reading pages, at 16px with 24-28px line height, inside Butterick's 45-90 character range ([Practical Typography](https://practicaltypography.com/line-length.html)). One saturated accent is typical (Brittany mint, Tania coral, Mitchell green, Addy yellow), and warm tints replace pure black and white on Lee's, Emil's, Delba's and Sara Soueidan's sites. Delivery varies far more. Lower-bound page weights ran from 68 KB (Simon Willison) and 74 KB (Sam Rose) to 3,279 KB (Nanda Syahrasyad), so weight does not separate acclaimed sites from each other.

Three absences are more useful than the convergences. **Resumes are visible from only 3 of 20 home pages** (Brittany, Maxime and Tania). Command palettes barely exist on these sites: a static scan found `cmdk` or ⌘K only on paco.me, as a project name, though a JS-only palette could be missed. Most importantly, **reach or output numbers appear on 4 of 20 sites, and business or product outcome numbers on none of the pages read**. The closest is Hamish Williams's qualitative outcome, the Pearson acquisition ([case study](https://hamishw.com/projects/smart-sparrow)). Most individual case-study pages went unopened, so this is a finding about home and index pages. It still marks a clear opening, because what these sites do show is what reviewers trust least: Marlow and Dabbish's employer interviews rated watchers and forks as unreliable popularity signals, with one employer saying it "kind of depends on how good a marketer that person was" ([CSCW 2013](https://www.cs.cmu.edu/~xia/resources/Documents/Marlow-cscw13.pdf)). That study is small and old, but its logic of trusting costly-to-fake signals has aged well.

Stacks say more about community than quality. **Ten of the 16 personal sites with an identifiable stack run Next.js**, all ten confirmed by page fingerprint, and most sit on Vercel, shipping about 148-249 KB of gzipped JS on the home page where measured. The sample is skewed towards that community. The cheaper alternatives prove the point: Tania's Gatsby site ships 70 KB, and Cassidy Williams's Astro site ships no first-party JS beyond analytics ([cassidoo.co](https://cassidoo.co)). None of the sampled sites uses Angular.

## AI made polish cheap, and several exemplar signatures are now tells

Trust in polished artefacts has fallen. Orosz's interviewees report that "CVs are high-quality, but the people behind them are not" ([Pragmatic Engineer](https://newsletter.pragmaticengineer.com/p/tech-jobs-market-in-2026-part-3-hiring)). Reddit hirers now say "a portfolio would be trivial to generate too" ([snapshot](https://reddit.sentinel-team.org/posts/1t4gswl/snapshots/2026-05-05T19%3A04%3A38.345544Z)). Experienced developers are the most sceptical readers of AI output, with 2.6% highly trusting it and 20% highly distrusting it ([Stack Overflow 2025](https://survey.stackoverflow.co/2025/ai)). That survey measures trust in tools, not candidates, but these are the peers reading the site. Hiring is moving towards explaining trade-offs and debugging AI-generated code live ([CoderPad](https://coderpad.io/blog/hiring-developers/new-research-the-2026-state-of-tech-hiring-what-ai-means-for-developers-and-hiring-teams/)). A good-looking site used to imply effort, and it no longer does.

The default look is now measurable. Adrian Krebs ran deterministic DOM and CSS checks on **1,590 Show HN pages: 22% matched four or more AI design patterns, 32% two or three, and 46% zero or one**, with false positives of "maybe 5-10%" ([Krebs](https://adriankrebs.ch/blog/design-slop/)). The patterns include Inter everywhere, a serif italic accent word in the hero, permanent dark mode with grey text and all-caps labels, gradients and glows, numbered steps and stat banner rows. Paul Bakaus's 68-entry impeccable catalogue adds soft spotlights, decorative grid backgrounds, tiny numbered section labels, decorative blinking cursors, pulsing status dots and "dark mode with glowing accents" ([impeccable.style](https://impeccable.style/slop?cb=30)). Neither source tests a hiring effect. Krebs measured launch pages, not portfolios, and the catalogue is opinion with no stated validation.

The awkward finding is how many exemplar signatures appear on these lists. Inter is on five of the 20 core sites. Paco's Newsreader italic phrase is the serif accent. Brittany's cursor spotlight matches the soft spotlight, Hamish's numbered projects sit close to numbered labels, and Jhey's and Nanda's faint grids resemble decorative grid backgrounds. These sites are not the problem; several predate the look or use the device with intent. **Copying them in 2026 imports a default**, so a serif-italic accent is no longer the cheap, durable idea it once was. Swapping names does not help either, because the catalogue also flags Geist and cream palettes: steering away from one default lands on the next. Linear still uses Inter, splitting Inter Display for headings from regular Inter for text ([Linear](https://linear.app/now/how-we-redesigned-the-linear-ui)), which shows that use matters more than choice. The catalogue keys on devices being decorative, so a label or number that carries meaning stays an ordinary typographic tool.

The remedy is specificity a template cannot supply. Long-dated artefacts such as multi-year commit histories and old posts are hard to backfill. Accepted contributions to high-status projects were rated reliable by Marlow and Dabbish's employers, who went to the project with the most commits and read the code. A write-up then becomes the core asset, and the best-supported shape combines NN/g's survey of 204 UX hiring leads, CoderPad's trade-off shift and practitioner advice, though it is a synthesis, not a tested template. It gives the business problem, scope in specifics, what you personally did against what the team did, the constraints, the options rejected and why, the outcome, what went wrong, and a checkable link. NN/g's respondents asked for exactly the rejected-options step: "I'm curious to know what isn't in the design and why" ([NN/g](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/ux-design-portfolios/)). Every number should sit beside a mechanism and a role, because a bare metric reads like the inflated claims hirers already distrust. Three deep items beat a long list: NN/g recommends 3-5 case studies for UX, and one guide suggests 3-6 substantive projects ([techinterview.org](https://www.techinterview.org/post/3233474627/personal-website-portfolio-engineers/)). For NDA-bound work, architecture explainers and decision retrospectives are the usual advice, on opinion only ([Standout](https://standout.work/blog/portfolio-for-senior-software-engineers)). Each claim is a promise for a live conversation, so publish nothing you cannot defend out loud.

## The rebuild starts with failures a reviewer would find in minutes

The current site fails the work-sample test before design enters into it. Rechecked on 2026-11-05: [/contact](https://nicholaspsmith.com/contact), /experience, /projects, /resume/ and /sitemap.xml all return **404**, and `https://www.nicholaspsmith.com` fails TLS with a certificate error. A shared deep link therefore lands on GitHub's error page, and the initial HTML is an empty `<app-root>` with no description or Open Graph tags (`frontend/src/index.html`), so link previews and non-JS crawlers get a title at best. Vercel and MERJ found that GPTBot, ClaudeBot and PerplexityBot do not execute JS, as of December 2024 ([Vercel](https://vercel.com/blog/the-rise-of-the-ai-crawler)). The live [robots.txt](https://nicholaspsmith.com/robots.txt) is also Cloudflare-managed and disallows GPTBot and ClaudeBot, which should be a deliberate choice for someone who wants to be found. Since forwarding is the site's main job, prerendering is the highest-value repair.

The other failures are visible to anyone with DevTools. The [background video](https://nicholaspsmith.com/matrix-background.mp4) is **45,150,174 bytes, roughly 390 times the site's 115 KB of gzipped JS**, and it loads automatically after a preview with no `saveData` or visibility check (`frontend/src/app/app.ts`). `frontend/src` contains no `prefers-reduced-motion` handling and no pause control, while WCAG 2.2.2 at Level A requires a mechanism for automatic motion over five seconds ([W3C](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/pause-stop-hide.html)); a purely decorative layer can be argued either way, but a visible toggle ends the argument. The resume renders as a JPG with three words of alt text (`frontend/src/app/resume/resume.html`). White on #04c766 is **2.24:1**, failing even the 3:1 large-text minimum, while near-black on the same green gives 8.49:1 (own WCAG calculation). The 50% overlay can let #e2e8f0 text fall to 2.48:1 over a white glyph, before counting any content box beneath it and depending on how bright the rain heads are (`frontend/src/app/app.css`). The contact form sends through FormSubmit with a personal Gmail address in the bundle (`frontend/src/app/contact/contact.ts`). And [nicksmith.software](https://nicksmith.software/) is a separate live portfolio, which the repo README links instead of this one, so two sites compete for one name. That choice is Nick's, and it should come before any design work.

The stack decision follows from the content. **Astro is the best fit if a rewrite is acceptable**: zero first-party JS by default, content collections for case studies, and islands for the few interactive parts ([Astro](https://docs.astro.build/en/concepts/why-astro/)). Angular 22's `outputMode: static` is the smallest migration and fixes the empty HTML and broken deep links ([Angular](https://angular.dev/guide/ssr)), but a blank app still ships about 72 KB brotli (scratch build). Choose it only if Angular roles are the target, where a fast static Angular site would stand out in a field that does not use it. Next `output: 'export'` is the weakest option: it keeps a React runtime and loses image optimisation, headers and redirects ([Next.js](https://nextjs.org/docs/app/guides/static-exports)). Hosting on Cloudflare Workers static assets removes the unverified origin hop and puts headers in the repo ([Cloudflare](https://developers.cloudflare.com/workers/static-assets/headers/)). Its 25 MiB file limit ([Cloudflare](https://developers.cloudflare.com/workers/platform/limits/)) forces the video decision anyway.

Performance targets should stay honest. The official thresholds remain LCP 2.5 s, INP 200 ms and CLS 0.1 at the 75th percentile ([web.dev](https://web.dev/articles/vitals)), and the circulating claim of a 2.0 s LCP in 2026 traces to blogs, with Google's own pages showing no change ([Search Central](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/core-web-vitals)). Google also says a perfect Lighthouse 100 is not expected ([Chrome docs](https://developer.chrome.com/docs/lighthouse/performance/performance-scoring)). A self-imposed budget of about 500 KB total first load and 95+ lab scores is judgement, well below the 2,559 KB median mobile home page ([Web Almanac 2025](https://almanac.httparchive.org/en/2025/page-weight)). No evidence ties load time to hiring outcomes; treat speed as a free sample of how you ship.

The rain is worth keeping because it is the site's strongest identity, but as stock video it reads as borrowed. Rezmason's MIT-licensed renderer holds the key idea: glyphs sit in a fixed grid and a raindrop is a wave of illumination passing through them, computed in small GPU textures ([Rezmason/matrix](https://github.com/Rezmason/matrix/blob/master/README.md)). A portfolio version should paint a static poster first so the rain is never the LCP element, start on idle, cap at 20-30 fps and a device pixel ratio of 1.5, stop when hidden, and show a still under reduced motion or without WebGL. A personal glyph set drawn from commit hashes or the site's own text makes it his, and a "bullet time" freeze would double as the required pause control. Readability should come from layout: near-opaque reading surfaces (9.33:1 at 0.92 opacity over a white glyph) and rain that quietens past the hero. One caution: no controlled benchmark compares video, Canvas and WebGL, and an unthrottled 60 fps shader on every page may be worse for battery than hardware-decoded video, so measure on real devices.

The raw material for the missing outcomes already exists. The 2026 resume records leading engineers across three continents to migrate mongodb.com from an in-house CMS to Contentstack, and a Cisco SecureX threat-context proof of concept that leadership greenlit as a standalone product (`frontend/public/resume/Nicholas_Smith_Resume_2026.pdf`). Both are named-employer stories that could take the case-study shape above, subject to what can be shared. The same resume gives the current role as Data Engineer, so the first screen has to frame that plainly for a frontend-focused search; Delba's and Emil Kowalski's sites both show that one calm, direct sentence is enough ([delba.dev](https://delba.dev), [emilkowal.ski](https://emilkowal.ski)).

## Conclusion

The evidence sorts this work by cost in an unexpected way. Everything with even indirect support (titles, dates, working links, plain structure, a real resume) is close to free, and everything expensive (signature motion, WebGL, custom type) has no evidence for hiring effect at all. That does not make craft worthless for a frontend engineer, because the site is the sample and peers are the likely route to a referral, but it sets the order: credibility first, then one idea. The most underrated decision is the content model. A single structured resume source that renders the page, the PDF, the JSON-LD and the experience list makes staleness structurally harder, and neglect is the failure that recurs among the exemplars.

The largest unknown is whether quantified, attributed outcomes change how reviewers respond, since no source tests it and no exemplar page reviewed tries it. A rebuilt site can run a small version of that experiment itself. Cookieless analytics ([Cloudflare Web Analytics](https://developers.cloudflare.com/web-analytics/faq/) by default, or [Umami](https://umami.is/pricing) if resume-download events matter) would show whether anyone reaches the case studies. Sparse traffic will not prove much, but it replaces the folklore on this topic with at least one honest data point, and the site itself would be a stronger exemplar for having one.
